# Digital PDF products that sell (2024-2026): formats, prices, niches, platforms. Fact base for a French-speaking PDF-guide seller (19-49 EUR)

*Labels: **[MEASURED]** stated method and sample. **[PLATFORM]** figure a platform states about itself (aggregate, not audited). **[ESTIMATE]** third-party scraped or modelled estimate. **[OPINION]** blog, vendor or guru claim without a verifiable method. **[OLD]** data from before 2024. **[LAW]** legal text as summarised by legal guides. Research date: 5 Nov 2026. Prices are in the source's currency; no FX conversion applied.*

## 1. Which formats sell most, at what price, and how bundling changes order value

### Takeaway
Downloadable products are the volume engine of creator storefronts (Stan: 2.2M download units in 2025 against 300K course units) at an average price of about $51, while Etsy planners and printables cluster at a $5-10 median with most listings under $10. A 19-49 EUR PDF guide therefore matches creator-storefront and funnel pricing, not typical Etsy pricing; products under $10 are a third of Gumroad's catalogue but under 1% of its modelled revenue. Order bumps and upsells are the documented order-value levers: +10-30% in non-vendor sources, higher in vendor claims.

### Cited Findings
**Creator storefronts (selling direct to an audience)**
- [PLATFORM] Stan, 2025 data from 80,000+ creators (aggregated, anonymised platform data plus a survey): average sale $67; average price by type: digital downloads $51, courses $96, memberships $61, webinars $68, community $33, 1:1 meetings $191. Page updated 21 Jul 2026 — [Stan, State of the Creator Economy 2026](https://stan.store/blog/state-of-the-creator-economy/)
- [PLATFORM] Units sold on Stan in 2025: digital downloads 2.2M, courses 300K, memberships 236K — [Stan](https://stan.store/blog/state-of-the-creator-economy/)
- [PLATFORM] Kajabi creator survey (n=1,717, run with Qualtrics, published Apr 2025): digital-download sales up 20% year on year as a revenue stream; memberships +10%; podcasts +47%; brand deals -52%; affiliate -36%; on-platform payouts -33% — [Tubefilter on Kajabi State of Creator Commerce](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/04/18/creator-entrepreneur-revenue-stream-kajabi-state-of-creator-commerce/amp)
- [ESTIMATE] Gumroad crawl of 146,271 products, revenue modelled as listed price x sales proxy (period unknown): courses average $95.74, described as 3.2x digital downloads; Self-Improvement average $26.67; Software Development $39.95 — [InsightRaider, Gumroad statistics](https://insightraider.com/en/data/gumroad-statistics-2026)
- [ESTIMATE] Same dataset: products under $10 are about 35% of products but 0.8% of revenue; the $30-49 band is said to convert 28% better than under-$10 products (method not explained) — [InsightRaider](https://insightraider.com/en/data/gumroad-statistics-2026)

- [OPINION] Templates (Notion, Canva, spreadsheets) have the highest unit volume on Gumroad; courses and ebooks earn well at $15-50. No dataset cited — [The Elite Engines](https://theeliteengines.com/answers/what-sells-best-on-gumroad.html)

**Bundles, order bumps, upsells**
- [PLATFORM, vendor] SamCart (cites $7B+ processed): order bumps convert 30-40% on average and sellers using them see 30%+ higher average order value — [SamCart](https://www.samcart.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-order-bumps); one-click upsells: 68% AOV lift — [SamCart](https://www.samcart.com/blog/upsell-strategies)
- [OPINION, vendors] CartFlows: 10-30% AOV lift from bumps — [CartFlows](https://cartflows.com/blog/order-bump/); Data Driven Marketing: 20-60% of buyers accept a well-built bump, cart value about +15%, upsell acceptance about 10-20% for digital products — [Data Driven Marketing](https://datadrivenmarketing.co/blog/order-bumps-vs-upsells/); Kajabi: post-purchase upsells convert 15-25% and lift AOV 20-40% — [Kajabi](https://www.kajabi.com/blog/digital-product-upsells-and-bundles)
- [PLATFORM] Systeme.io, the main French-built funnel tool, lists sales funnels, order bumps, one-click upsells, an affiliate programme and coupon codes in its plan table — [Systeme.io tarifs](https://systeme.io/fr/pricing)

### Inferences
- On Stan, downloads are about 80% of the units of the three reported types (2.2M of 2.74M). Units x average price gives roughly $112M for downloads, $29M for courses and $14M for memberships, so downloads probably lead gross revenue too. This is my calculation, not Stan's, and membership "units" may not be single payments.
- Stan's average sale ($67) is above its average download price ($51). That fits a mix of higher-priced items and/or multi-item carts; Stan gives no breakdown.
- A 19-49 EUR guide sits near the creator-storefront average ($51) and the Gumroad "$30-49 converts better" claim, but at 2-5x the Etsy planner median. On Etsy it needs visible differentiation (bundle, narrow niche, depth) to hold that price.
- Courses sell at about 2x the price of downloads on both Stan ($96 vs $51) and Gumroad ($95.74 vs about $30 implied by the 3.2x ratio), so a PDF + video "mini-course" is the documented route to a higher ticket.
- For planning, assume +10-30% AOV from a bump (non-vendor ranges). Treat SamCart's 30%+ and 68% as upper bounds, given selection bias (sellers who adopted the tool).

### Gaps
- No measured price distribution per format (workbook, checklist, kit, printable planner, GoodNotes planner, Notion template) with a stated method. RankHero samples are small (57-60 listings) and its method is undisclosed.
- No measured data on how bundles change AOV on Etsy or Gumroad; all bump and upsell figures come from checkout vendors.
- No reliable share of PDF guides (as opposed to templates) within digital downloads on any platform.

## 2. Largest and fastest-growing niches; evergreen versus trend-driven

### Takeaway
No source measures niche size for PDF products directly. The best proxies put Writing & Publishing, Fitness & Health, Business & Money and Self-Improvement at the top of non-software categories by modelled revenue per product on Gumroad, with Education and Gaming far behind. On Etsy, broad planner keywords are saturated (hundreds of listings per monthly search) while a specific life-event keyword such as wedding planning has about 20x fewer listings per search. Annual life-event flows in France (about 644k births and 251k marriages in 2025) make parenting and weddings evergreen; planners are seasonal, peaking in January with a second peak in late summer.

### Inferences
- Listings per monthly search (my calculation from the RankHero figures): printable planner about 360-460, digital planner pdf about 520-700, wedding planner printable about 19-25. A specific audience tied to a life event faces an order of magnitude less competition.
- Evergreen (recurring annual flows or lasting needs): parenting and birth (about 644k births a year in France), weddings (about 251k a year), pets (about 9.5M identified dogs), personal finance and budgeting, health and fitness, self-employment admin. Trend-driven or perishable: year-dated planners (re-issued every year, January and back-to-school peaks), tool-bound formats (GoodNotes, Notion, Canva), seasonal designs (Halloween).
- Fitness & Health combines high revenue per product with the lowest category rating (3.36/5): demand is strong but promises disappoint. Calibrated claims matter there, and it is also a French regulatory risk (section 5).
- Births are falling and marriages slightly rising in France: the parenting pool shrinks slowly, which caps growth but is no reason to avoid the niche.

### Gaps
- No measured market size or growth by niche (parenting, pets, personal finance, wedding, studying/exams, travel, side hustles, home organisation, mental health, self-employment admin) for PDF products on any platform; Etsy publishes no category split for digital items.
- No French-language keyword volumes found (e.g., "planning mariage a imprimer"); the RankHero data is English-language and mostly US.
- No data found for the studying/exams, travel or home-organisation niches (a search for national French bac candidate counts returned only regional and foreign figures).
- No measured trend data on Notion or Canva template demand.

## 3. Platform facts (Etsy, Gumroad, Stan, Payhip, Beacons, Systeme.io, Podia, Kajabi, TikTok Shop, Amazon KDP)

### Takeaway
Etsy is large but flat (Q4 2025 marketplace GMS $3.29B, +0.1%; 86.5M active buyers, -3.4%) and costs a France-based seller about 13-14% of a 20-39 EUR digital sale, rising to about 28-29% when Offsite Ads apply. Creator storefronts differ mainly by fee model: Gumroad 10% + $0.50 (30% via Discover) as merchant of record; Payhip 5%/2%/0% by plan with EU/UK VAT handled; Stan $29-99/month with no platform fee; Beacons 9% on free plans; Systeme.io as the French all-in-one with bumps and upsells. TikTok Shop does not accept ordinary PDF downloads (invite-only Virtual Goods in the US; digital downloads unsupported in the EU), and KDP "low-content" means printed journals and planners, not PDFs.

### Inferences
- Etsy fee stack for a France-based seller (my calculation with the rates above; VAT on fees excluded): a 20 EUR sale costs 1.30 + 1.10 + 0.18 + 0.23 = 2.81 EUR (about 14%); a 39 EUR sale costs 5.02 EUR (about 12.9%). With Offsite Ads at 15%: 5.81 EUR (about 29%) and 10.87 EUR (about 28%).
- Break-evens (my calculation): Payhip Plus beats Free above about $967/month in sales, and Pro beats Plus above about $3,500/month. At a $40 price Gumroad takes $4.50 per sale, so Stan's $29/month is cheaper from about 7 sales a month. Beacons free (9%) beats a $29 flat fee below about $322/month.
- For EU buyers, a merchant-of-record or VAT-handling platform (Gumroad, Payhip) removes the OSS VAT admin; for Stan and Systeme.io the tax handling was not confirmed.
- Stan's averages imply modest volumes: at $51 per download, creators under 1K followers make about 1.7 sales a month and those over 100K about 27 (rough; assumes all revenue comes from downloads).
- In France, TikTok is a traffic channel for PDFs, not a checkout.
- KDP low-content is a physical spin-off channel (printed versions of a planner or journal), not a PDF channel.

### Gaps
- Etsy's official fee page returned HTTP 403; fees come from French guides and third-party reports of Etsy announcements. Etsy publishes no digital-download share, category size or best-seller review statistics (none found).
- Shopify digital-product data, Podia's current fees and Systeme.io plan prices were not retrieved.
- TikTok's own Seller Center policy page was not reached; all TikTok facts are secondary and partly inconsistent.
- A search excerpt credits InsightRaider with a "median creator earns about $72/month" figure, but the same page warns that its product estimates do not measure monthly creator income. Not usable.

## 4. What best-selling listings have in common

### Takeaway
The only quantified patterns are extreme concentration of sales, high ratings (4.4+ stars) among top earners, avoidance of sub-$10 pricing, tiered offers, and above all audience size, which Stan's own data and leadership call the binding constraint. On page counts, "done-for-you" promises, editable vs fixed files, bonuses or number of items per bundle, I found no measured data, only vendor advice.

### Cited Findings
- [ESTIMATE] Extreme concentration: the top 130 of 12,952 Gumroad products with sales make 77.3% of modelled revenue; downloads have a median of about 30 lifetime sales against a mean of 293 — [InsightRaider](https://insightraider.com/en/data/gumroad-statistics-2026)
- [ESTIMATE] Top revenue earners hold 4.4+ stars "nearly without exception"; ratings below 3.5 correlate with $0 lifetime revenue. Claims about cover images and description length are correlations with no stated method — [InsightRaider](https://insightraider.com/en/data/gumroad-statistics-2026)
- [ESTIMATE] Sub-$10 products: 35% of products, 0.8% of revenue; 2-3 price tiers bring about 2x revenue — [InsightRaider](https://insightraider.com/en/data/gumroad-statistics-2026)
- [PLATFORM] Revenue rises steeply with audience: average monthly sales go from $89 (under 1K followers) to $1,378 (100K+) — [Stan](https://stan.store/blog/state-of-the-creator-economy/). Stan's co-founder sums it up: "It's about who knows you" — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/stan-helped-creators-make-500m-selling-digital-products-its-biggest-lesson-was-that-products-arent-the-problem/)
- [OLD 2022] Growing an audience was the top creator challenge (32.9%); product creation was only 8.86% — [Podia](https://podia.com/articles/creator-survey-results)
- [OPINION, vendor, unsourced] On Etsy, listings under $20 convert with 5-10 reviews, while $20-50 listings need 15-30 (search excerpt; exact page not pinned) — [Insight Agent](https://www.insightagent.app/guides/etsy-digital-product-benchmarks-2026)
- [OPINION] Planner keywords work best when they pair type and format (e.g., "printable budget planner") rather than "planner" alone — [Listify AI](https://www.listifyai.net/blog/etsy-keywords-for-printable-planners)
- [ESTIMATE] Specific keywords face far fewer competing listings: "wedding planner printable" 19-25k listings vs 0.87-1.1M for "printable planner" — [RankHero](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/wedding-planner-printable), [RankHero](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/printable-planner)
- [OPINION, anecdote] One seller's refund rates ranged from 30% down to under 3.5% across three products, depending on copy, delivery and fast early wins — [Typeshare, Blake Wyatt](https://typeshare.co/blakewyatt/posts/7-rules-for-online-course-creators-to-stick-the-sale-and-achieve-sub-5percent-refund-rates-at-scale)

### Inferences
- Patterns best supported by the data: (1) price around $30 or more with a tiered ladder (basic, complete, bundle) rather than under $10; (2) protecting the rating (4.4+) is non-negotiable; (3) a narrow audience plus a life-event or problem keyword to escape saturation; (4) building audience and traffic before widening the catalogue.
- "Done-for-you" and editable formats may explain why templates lead unit volume (Gumroad opinion source), but this is not measured.

### Gaps
- No measured study links page count, number of items in a bundle, bonuses, editable vs fixed files or "done-for-you" promises to sales on any platform.
- No Etsy data on typical review counts of best-sellers or on Bestseller badge criteria; EverBee or eRank exports would be needed.

## 5. French-speaking market specifics (France, Belgium, Switzerland, Quebec)

### Takeaway
No measured size exists for the infoproduit market in France, Belgium, Switzerland or Quebec; the available figures are blog estimates without method (about 150,000 French-speaking infopreneurs, most earning little). Adjacent measured data: French digital publishing is about 279M EUR (about 9.6% of publishers' revenue, flat), and 82% of Quebec adults bought online in 2025 (a record), while three-quarters of Quebec internet users have cut purchases from US merchants. Systeme.io is the dominant French-built tool; French regulators actively target misleading influencer and wellness-coaching claims; and the withdrawal right on downloads is lost only with the buyer's express consent and waiver.

### Cited Findings
**Market size and creators**
- [OPINION, OLD 2023, no method] About 150,000 French-speaking infopreneurs; about 90% of the market makes very little (search excerpt) — [The B Boost, 2023](https://www.thebboost.fr/avenir-marche-infopreneuriat/)
- [OPINION, no source] French infopreneurs average 2,400 EUR/month in revenue; 80% earn under 1,000 EUR/month gross (search excerpt; attribution between this blog and the one above not fully certain) — [Boris Foucaud](https://www.consultant.borisfoucaud.com/infopreneur-mythes-realite/)
- [OPINION] A French trainer-marketer: "quick fix" products under 100 EUR; caution on asynchronous-only programmes at 500 EUR+; completion under 50% (no source); under 3% of learners reach the promised result for some trainers (anecdote). - [PLATFORM] Systeme.io claims 500,000+ users and 10,000+ infopreneurs; estimated revenue about $20.1M in 2024 (sources in section 3) — [GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/systemeio), [Systeme.io blog](https://systeme.io/fr/blog/devenir-infopreneur)

**Adjacent consumer data**
- [MEASURED] French digital publishing revenue in 2024: 278.6M EUR (-1.6%); total publisher revenue 2.902bn EUR (-1.5%), so digital is about 9.6% (my calculation) — [ENSSIB, L'edition en perspective 2024-2025](https://www.enssib.fr/bibliotheque-numerique/documents/73428-l-edition-en-perspective-2024-2025.pdf), [Livres Hebdo](https://www.livreshebdo.fr/article/ledition-resiste-dans-un-contexte-difficile); [OLD 2021] digital was 9.3% of publishers' book sales — [SNE synthese 2021](https://www.livreshebdo.fr/sites/default/files/assets/document/rs21_synthese_web.pdf)

- [MEASURED] Three-quarters of Quebec internet users reduced or stopped online purchases from US merchants (Universite Laval release, May 2025; headline only read) — [Universite Laval](https://salledepresse.ulaval.ca/2025/05/08/les-trois-quarts-des-internautes-du-quebec-ont-reduit-ou-cesse-leurs-achats-en-ligne-aupres-de-marchands-americains-87cba1b5-b4a5-4891-9e35-ee869ab30b24); [OLD 2023] Quebec adults spent nearly C$18bn online in 2023 — [Universite Laval](https://salledepresse.ulaval.ca/2024/04/10/les-adultes-du-quebec-ont-depense-pres-de-18-milliards-de-pour-des-achats-en-ligne-lan-dernier-a:64ab5823-6dbe-452c-a7f1-cc8d758e0bbb)

**Regulation and consumer protection (France, EU)**
- [MEASURED, 2022-2023 checks] The DGCCRF checked about sixty influencers and agencies since 2021; 60% of influencers checked broke advertising rules. Misleading commercial practices are punishable by up to 2 years in prison and a 300,000 EUR fine — [economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/actualites/influenceurs-bilan-des-controles-2022-et-2023-de-la-dgccrf), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/france/advertising-marketing--branding/1327402/pratiques-commerciales-trompeuses--six-influenceurs-%C3%A9pingl%C3%A9s-par-la-dgccrf)
- [MEASURED, OLD 2021-2022] DGCCRF "wellness coaching" probe (including online practices and training centres): anomalies at nearly 80% of professionals checked, about 20% misleading practices — [DGCCRF](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/dgccrf/actualites-dgccrf/secteur-du-coaching-bien-etre-lenquete-de-la-dgccrf-releve-80-danomalies-chez-les-professionnels-controles)
- [LAW] Art. L221-28 13 of the Code de la consommation: digital content not supplied on a physical medium loses the withdrawal right only if delivery started after the consumer's express prior consent and express waiver. A pre-ticked box is not enough; the consent must be confirmed on a durable medium (L221-13); conformity rights remain — [LegalPlace](https://www.legalplace.fr/guides/droit-retractation-internet/), [Donnees Personnelles](https://donneespersonnelles.fr/contenu-numerique-acces-immediat)
- [OPINION, unverified] A transposition (ordonnance 2026-2 and decret 2026-3 of 5 Jan 2026, in force 19 June 2026) touched L221-28 alongside an EU "withdrawal button" — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/fr/bouton-retractation-jeux-video-2026/)
- [OPINION, secondary] TikTok Shop in the EU does not support digital downloads (section 3)

### Inferences
- With no measured market size, the French-speaking opportunity has to be sized from life-event flows (births, marriages, pets; section 2) and from platform data.
- French readers already pay for ebooks (about 279M EUR a year), but that market is flat; growth for PDF guides will come from creator-direct sales (social media plus funnel), not bookshops.
- Quebec buyers shop online at record levels but are turning away from US merchants, so a French-speaking, non-US brand may have an edge there (untested).
- Health, wellbeing and money promises are exactly what the DGCCRF targets: guide marketing must avoid unproven outcome claims and fake urgency or discounts.
- The checkout must collect an express, unticked consent and waiver before delivery and confirm it by email; otherwise the 14-day withdrawal right stays.

### Gaps
- No measured size, price survey or consumer-expectation study for infoproduits in France, Belgium, Switzerland or Quebec (searches for FEVAD, Xerfi and press coverage found nothing specific).
- The NETendances 2025 PDF could not be parsed (compressed PDF; no local Python or poppler), so any digital-product line items in it were not extracted.
- No data on platform shares among French sellers (Systeme.io vs Etsy vs Gumroad vs Payhip vs Learnybox or Schoolmaker).
- No relevant data found for Belgium or Switzerland, nor on Swiss or Canadian tax handling for digital sales.
- No source on typical French prices for PDF guides in the 19-49 EUR band.

## 6. Refund and satisfaction patterns

### Takeaway
Published refund benchmarks (vendor-level, no audited datasets) put flat downloads at about 1-5% and courses much higher (4-30%), against about 1.4% for physical e-commerce. The sources point to expectation gaps, a slow first result and over-promising categories as the drivers (lowest ratings in fitness and health, and photography); no data ties refund rates to price bands.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION, vendor] Flat downloads 1-4%; courses 4-12%; live programmes higher — [SendOwl](https://www.sendowl.com/blog/tips-and-advice/course-vs-digital-download-decision-framework)
- [OPINION] Digital products 2-5% — [Kissmetrics](https://kissmetrics.io/glossary/refund-rate); 1-2% described as healthy (apparently SaaS-derived) — [Fungies](https://fungies.io/digital-product-refunds-2026-guide/)
- [OPINION] Courses and training 10-25% (industry estimate) — [Count.co](https://count.co/metric/refund-rate); "industry average" up to 30% while the author's own courses stay under 2% — [Simpleseven](https://simpleseven.com/preventing-handling-online-course-refund-requests/)
- [OPINION, anecdote] One seller saw 30% down to under 3.5% across three products, depending on copy, delivery and quick early wins — [Typeshare, Blake Wyatt](https://typeshare.co/blakewyatt/posts/7-rules-for-online-course-creators-to-stick-the-sale-and-achieve-sub-5percent-refund-rates-at-scale)
- [MEASURED, physical comparator] WooCommerce stores averaged about 1.4% refunds in 2025 — [Metorik](https://metorik.com/blog/ecommerce-refund-rate-benchmarks)
- [PLATFORM, policy example] Follett: ebooks refundable within 14 days of access (30 days if never opened), refused if more than 10% has been viewed — [Follett](https://support.follett.com/hc/en-us/articles/33298465698196-What-is-the-return-policy-for-online-purchases)
- [ESTIMATE] Lowest buyer ratings on Gumroad: Fitness & Health 3.36/5, Photography 2.6/5; under 3.5 stars correlates with $0 revenue — [InsightRaider](https://insightraider.com/en/data/gumroad-statistics-2026)
- [OPINION] Completion of online trainings is under 50% (no source given) — [The B Boost, 2026](https://www.thebboost.fr/marche-formation-en-ligne-2026/)
- [LAW] In France, downloads lose the withdrawal right only with express consent and waiver (section 5) — [LegalPlace](https://www.legalplace.fr/guides/droit-retractation-internet/)

### Inferences
- For a 19-49 EUR guide (a download, not a course), plan for about 2-5% refunds; a higher rate signals a mismatch between promise and content.
- The levers the sources name are a precise promise, instant delivery, a quick win in the first pages and a compliant waiver flow (French law), rather than a no-refund stance.

### Gaps
- No measured refund rate by price band or by format (checklist vs guide vs planner), no survey of refund reasons, and no Etsy, Gumroad or Stan refund or chargeback statistics.
