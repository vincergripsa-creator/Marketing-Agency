# Product visuals that sell digital PDF guides: covers, mockups, listing images, previews (2024-2026)

Labels used on every claim: [MEASURED] = test or study with a stated method and sample; [SURVEY] = self-reported buyer or creator data; [PLATFORM RULE] = official Etsy, Amazon or Gumroad documentation; [PLATFORM DATA] = a platform's own usage data with no baseline disclosed; [PRACTITIONER] = designer or seller opinion; [ANECDOTE] = one uncontrolled case; [VENDOR] = the source sells the tool or service it praises; [OLDER yyyy] = data from before 2024. Research done 5 Nov 2026. Several etsy.com Seller Handbook pages and canva.com returned HTTP 403, so some Etsy and Canva details come from search excerpts or press coverage; this is flagged where it applies.

## 1. Cover design: what makes an ebook or guide cover sell (thumbnail legibility, title, focal image, faces, colour, niche conventions), and is any of it measured?

### Takeaway
The only rule that is both consistent and partly measured is "win at thumbnail size": a large, high-contrast title and few elements. In the best documented test (BookBub Ads, cozy-mystery series, page updated 2024), typography-led covers beat image-led covers on click-through. In a Reedsy ad test, professional covers lifted CTR much less for nonfiction (+12.5%) than for fiction (+28% to +53%). I found no measured data on faces vs no faces, or on the conventions of each niche (parenting, finance, fitness, wedding, travel, study). The one sizeable eye-tracking study (n=180, 2018) shows that cover colour sways choice and that the preferred colours differ by sex and age.

### Cited Findings
**Size and legibility**
- [PLATFORM RULE] KDP's ideal ebook cover is 2,560 px tall × 1,600 px wide, a 1.6:1 height-to-width ratio. Height should be about 2,500 px for HD devices. — [KDP Help](https://KDP.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G6GTK3T3NUHKLEFX)
- [PRACTITIONER] Check covers at 100-160 px wide. Make the title the brightest, highest-contrast element. — [Creative Paramita](https://creativeparamita.com/book-cover/book-cover-thumbnail-that-survives-the-amazon-test/)
- [PRACTITIONER, OLDER 2017] Titles that can't be read at thumbnail size are a common problem and hurt discoverability. The claim rests on the author's own vetting of submissions, not on a study. — [Indies Unlimited](https://indiesunlimited.com/2017/11/21/the-case-for-legible-titles-book-covers-in-thumbnail/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Bold contrast and plain type read best when tiny. "If you struggle to read the title, that's a red flag." — [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/marketing-value-professional-book-cover/)
- [PRACTITIONER] A forum post says covers are often seen at about 100 px across. This is not a measurement. — [Writing Forums](https://www.writingforums.com/threads/cover-reactions-wanted.202491/)

**Measured cover tests (all small, all on ads, none on Etsy)**
- [MEASURED, one author, one genre] Tricia O'Malley, BookBub Partners blog, last updated 15 Aug 2024 (the test date is not given). Cozy-mystery series, low-budget BookBub Ads aimed at new readers:
  - Round 1: original 2.94% CTR vs new image cover 2.97%, about 6,000 impressions each.
  - Round 2: a completely different design lost by about 1 percentage point of CTR.
  - Round 3: the original plus a border reached 3.12% (217 clicks / 6,964 impressions), vs 2.85% for the original (162 / 5,684).
  - Wild-card round: a typography-heavy cover won clearly, at 5.2% CTR.
  - Book two: original 0.58% vs typography cover 1.21%, about 2,200 impressions each.
  - The author explains the win by contrast with the genre: cozy mysteries are full of witch hats, cats and knives, so a type-only cover stood out. After she rolled the typography design out across the series, free downloads of book 1 rose 40%. That figure is a before/after comparison, not controlled. — [BookBub Insights](https://insights.bookbub.com/boosted-series-sales-testing-cover-designs/)
- [MEASURED, OLDER 2021] Book Brush had five authors run BookBub Ads split tests, changing one image element each and running each variant to at least 2,000 impressions. The article gives no CTR numbers, only the winners:
  - A plain solid-colour background beat a background made of the enlarged cover.
  - A 3D cover shown on an iPad overtook the flat 2D cover over time.
  - Placing the cover on the left was the clear loser; centre and right were close.
  - A blurb vs no blurb was a near tie, and so was a button vs a text call to action. — [BookBub Insights](https://insights.bookbub.com/book-advertising-design-elements-clicks/)
- [MEASURED, undated, no significance test] Reedsy ran a one-week Facebook-ads A/B test on 4 self-published titles. Each ad pair differed only in the cover, and each variant reached about 1,000 users. Professional covers raised CTR by 35% on average: romance +48%, mystery/thriller +53%, YA fantasy +28%, nonfiction (*What Would a Muslim Say?*) only +12.5%. — [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/marketing-value-professional-book-cover/)
- [ANECDOTE/VENDOR, not verified in full] A cover-design studio reports one test where Cover B sold 4× more than Cover A. It credits clear genre signals, readable text and bold colours. — [Design Shifu](https://www.designshifu.com/blog/book-cover-ab-test-results)
- [PRACTITIONER, method not shown] Kindlepreneur's "Lessons Learned from 20,000 Self-Published Books" advises designing the cover like the current bestsellers in your category. — [Kindlepreneur](https://kindlepreneur.com/book-cover-design-lessons/)
- [PLATFORM DATA] PickFu cover polls usually use about 50 US respondents (some use 200). They can target readers by genre or reading frequency, and respondents explain their choice in writing. One 50-vote head-to-head was flagged as not statistically significant. — [PickFu book covers](https://www.pickfu.com/books/book-covers)

**Eye-tracking and attention**
- [MEASURED, OLDER 2018] Gudinavičius & Šuminas, *Journal of Documentation* 74(2), 2018. 180 participants, balanced by sex and split into three age bands, saw 18 random cover thumbnails on an eye-tracker screen and clicked the one they liked:
  - Cover colour partly drives choice.
  - Women aged 18-35 preferred cool colours; men aged 56+ preferred warm ones.
  - Both sexes liked orange and disliked yellow, green, black and white.
  - Men chose blue and red covers; women chose multicolour and violet ones.
  - For women only, time spent looking at a cover correlated with choosing it. — [Paper PDF, Vilnius University](https://www.kf.vu.lt/dokumentai/darbuotojai/Choosing_a_book_by_its_cover_JD-09-2016-0111.pdf)
- [MEASURED, OLDER, n=23] Sato et al. (Doshisha University) put eye trackers on 23 undergraduates choosing books from a shelf. Warm vs cool colour made no difference to gaze time. Gothic (sans-serif style) type held the gaze longer than Ming (serif style) type. Reported second-hand. — [Ooligan Press](https://www.ooliganpress.com/book-cover-design-from-a-scientific-approach/)
- [MEASURED, OLDER 2006] Nielsen Norman Group's early eye-tracking work, as reported by Poynter:
  - Users on a task largely ignore generic or stock images and images that look like ads.
  - Images related to the content get noticed, and faces attract attention. — [Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/news/what-makes-web-images-attractive)
- [MEASURED, 2022] In an eye-tracking study of product listings (Chocarro et al.), pictures are looked at first, before product names and prices. Over the whole viewing, they draw less total attention than the text. — [Universidad Pública de Navarra repository](https://academica-e.unavarra.es/handle/2454/44873)

**Genre conventions for nonfiction and self-help (all opinion)**
- [PRACTITIONER, Nov 2025] Typefaces common on self-help and personal-development covers: Futura, Avenir, Poppins, Open Sans, Raleway. Clean sans-serifs suit modern nonfiction. — [Self-Publishing School](https://self-publishingschool.com/book-cover-typography-guide/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Self-help covers lean toward soft tones and approachable type. Some of the most effective nonfiction covers use typography alone. — [Monkey C Media](https://monkeycmedia.com/nonfiction-cover-design-what-you-need-to-know)
- [PRACTITIONER] Follow the genre's standards while following trends. — [Miblart](https://miblart.com/blog/book-cover-trends-2023/)
- [PRACTITIONER] For nonfiction, research what the audience expects. Avoid stacked clichés, such as a script "wedding" font when the image already says wedding. — [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/marketing-value-professional-book-cover/)

### Inferences
- **Default to typography-first covers.** Three things point the same way: the BookBub typography wins, the small lift that imagery gave nonfiction in Reedsy's test, and the attention that sans-serif type held in the Sato study. An automated designer should start from a large title block (a few words, highest contrast) and allow at most one focal image.
- **Hard gate.** Shrink every cover to 150 px wide and to a phone-grid tile. If the title cannot be read, reject the cover.
- **Differentiate inside the convention.** O'Malley's typography cover won because her genre was saturated with imagery. The designer should scan the top results in the niche and use the visual axis competitors neglect (type-only, a colour block, or illustration), while keeping the niche's tone.
- **Audience colour (weak signal).** The 2018 study was a Lithuanian sample. If it transfers, a parenting audience that skews towards women aged 25-40 might respond better to cool, multicolour or violet palettes than to yellow, green, black or white. Treat this only as a hypothesis to test.
- **Statistical caution, my own calculation from the numbers above.** The border test (3.12% vs 2.85%) gives z ≈ 0.9, which is not significant. The book-two test (0.58% vs 1.21%, about 13 vs 27 clicks) gives z ≈ 2.2, p ≈ 0.03, which is significant but fragile. With CTRs of 1-3%, about 2,000 impressions per variant can only detect very large differences.

### Gaps
- I found no public data from Amazon KDP cover A/B tests and no aggregate PickFu study of cover traits.
- I found no measured data on faces vs no faces, on single focal image vs several, or on the conventions of the six niches (parenting, finance, fitness, wedding, travel, study).
- Some figures circulate without any source and should not be used: "genre-appropriate covers raise sales 30-50%" and "typography is about 60% of a cover's impact".

## 2. Design trends 2025-2026 for covers and digital product branding, and which read as premium vs cheap

### Takeaway
The 2026 forecasts converge on a reaction against polished, AI-looking sameness. That covers Canva (platform search data plus a survey of 1,000 creators), Adobe, and book-cover studios. Rising styles are collage and scrapbook, tactile texture, hand-made and oversized expressive type, zine and editorial layouts, and surreal imagery. A strong minimalist counter-current runs alongside: clean layouts, serif fonts, and an off-white Pantone 2026 colour. Measured evidence on premium vs cheap is thin and mostly older:
- White space signals prestige.
- Handwritten type helps hedonic products and hurts functional ones.
- Consumers think less of brands that use AI images of people or products (Getty, more than 30,000 adults, 2024).

### Cited Findings
**Canva Design Trends 2026** (published 11 Dec 2025). [PLATFORM DATA + SURVEY] Method: Canva design and search activity, its Designer Advisory Board, and a survey of 1,000 creators in the US and Brazil. The search-growth percentages below have no baseline. — [IT Voice summary](https://www.itvoice.in/canva-unveils-2026-design-trends-the-year-of-imperfect-by-design); [Mediaweek](https://mediaweek.com.au/data/why-designs-next-flex-is-being-imperfect); [Campaign Brief](https://campaignbrief.com/?p=305692). The canva.com report page returned 403.
- Reality Warp (real blended with surreal): searches for "liminal" and "uncanny" +220% year on year.
- Prompt Playground (UI fragments, retro-tech, emotion-first): "lo-fi aesthetic" searches +527%.
- Explorecore (calm zine and Substack-style layouts for slow reading): +85%.
- Texture Check (glossy, waxy and tactile, hyper-real materials): +30%.
- Notes App Chic (scrapbook, messy, behind-the-scenes): DIY and collage elements +90%.
- Opt-Out Era (minimal: clean layouts, serif fonts, simple branding): +54%.
- Drama Club (cinematic, high-emotion): +27%.
- Regional trends: GrannyWave (India), Zinegeist (Mexico; collaged layouts, matte textures, bold oversized type; "brutalist design" and "type poster" +77%), Block Party (Spain; folklore in warm vintage tones).
- Survey: 80% of creators said they would "regain creative control" in 2026; 77% called AI an "essential partner".

**Adobe**
- [PRACTITIONER, 12 Dec 2025, no data] Adobe Express names 10 trends: sensory maximalism with puffy, soft, squishy textures; exaggerated playful letters (oversized sans, bubbly or wavy type, loopy scripts); bright saturated colour mixing nostalgic and futuristic; surreal and absurdist collage; organic and imperfect (hand-rendered and letterpress type, earthy textures, low-contrast minimal forms); freeform zine-like editorial layouts; warm personal style; local and cultural heritage; layered collage (photos, doodles, stamps); maximalist chaotic layouts. Claymorphism, gradients, grain and risograph are not named; the nearest are the "puffy" textures and the nostalgic palettes. — [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/design-trends-2026)
- [PRACTITIONER, secondary coverage] Adobe's 2026 Creative Trends forecast has four themes centred on real, sensory, emotional content and a move away from over-polished, automated visuals. The named themes include "All the feels" and "Connectioneering". The forecast cites Kantar research saying brands seen as fun and playful build future demand better. — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/creative-inspiration/4-creative-trends-for-2026-according-to-adobe); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/adobe-forecasts-four-creative-trends-shaping-2026-advertising-campaigns/)

**Colour and other trend sources**
- [PRACTITIONER] Pantone Colour of the Year 2026 is PANTONE 11-4201 Cloud Dancer, a soft off-white, announced 4 Dec 2025. It is Pantone's first white, and the reception was mixed (called boring, even tone-deaf). — [Pantone release via Texintel](https://www.texintel.com/press-room/pantone-12-25-p-pantone-unveils-pantone-11-4201-cloud-dancer-as-color-of-the-year-2026-offering-a-sanctuary-of-calm-in-a-chaotic-world); [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/lifestyle/pantone-s-2026-color-of-the-year-is-cloud-dancer/story); [Design News Now](https://designnewsnow.com/?p=33687)
- Shutterstock's official creative-trends page still shows its 2022 report. I found no 2026 Shutterstock trend content. — [Shutterstock](https://content.shutterstock.com/creative-trends/)

**Book-cover trend pieces for 2026 (all [PRACTITIONER])**
- Hand-rendered and textured art (oil, charcoal), partly as a reaction to AI; over-exaggerated typography. — [Greenleaf](https://greenleafbookgroup.com/learning-center/book-creation/2026-cover-design-trends)
- "Typography takes centre stage"; more restraint and brand coherence. — [Troubador](https://troubador.co.uk/blog/book-cover-trends)
- A pullback from overly polished, digitally flawless design. — [Spine Magazine](https://spinemagazine.co/articles/book-cover-design-trends-in-2025-predictions-for-2026)
- Collage and mixed media, bold minimalism, nature themes. — [Publuu](https://publuu.com/knowledge-base/top-book-cover-design-trends/)
- AI-generated covers tend to try to summarise the book visually, which a cover should not do. — [Damonza](https://damonza.com/6-book-cover-design-trends-for-2026/)

**Premium vs cheap: measured evidence**
- [SURVEY, 2024; data 2022-2024] Getty Images VisualGPS "Building Trust in the Age of AI", more than 30,000 adults in 25 countries:
  - Nearly 90% want to know whether an image is AI-made or AI-modified.
  - 76% say they cannot tell whether an image is real.
  - 98% say authentic images are pivotal to trust.
  - More prefer real images (38%) than AI images (15%) in marketing.
  - Brands that use AI visuals of humans or products are viewed less favourably. AI images of non-human subjects are seen as less misleading.
  - Markets such as Southeast Asia, Latin America, the UAE and Saudi Arabia are more receptive to AI imagery.
  - Sources: [Getty Images press release](https://investors.gettyimages.com/news-releases/news-release-details/nearly-90-consumers-want-transparency-ai-images-finds-getty); [Report PDF](https://reports.gettyimages.com/VisualGPS-Building-Trust-In-AI.pdf)
- [MEASURED, OLDER 2018] Schroll, Schnurr & Grewal, *Journal of Consumer Research*. Handwritten typefaces create a sense of human presence, which improves product evaluations. In a field test, crisp-bread and chocolate packs with handwritten type sold more than the same packs set in machine type. The effect reversed for functional products: handwriting helped a decorative candle and hurt an insect-repelling one. Only physical packaging was tested. — [SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/marketing/handwritten-typefaces-can-increase-sales-indulgent-products/); [Science Says](https://app.sciencesays.com/p/use-handwritten-fonts-to-increase)
- [MEASURED/SURVEY, OLDER 2006-2013] Pracejus, Olsen & O'Guinn on white space in ads:
  - A summary says white-space ads make products seem more prestigious, trustworthy and higher quality; the underlying study is not named there. — [MarketingProfs](https://www.marketingprofs.com/short-articles/207/how-saying-the-least-may-achieve-the-most)
  - The 2013 *IJRM* paper finds the effect works as rhetoric, not as costly signalling, and that its meaning differs across cultures. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ijrema/v30y2013i3p211-218.html)
  - The 2012 *JBR* survey of 31 North American creative directors lists "convey brand prestige" among their reasons for using white space. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbrese/v65y2012i6p855-860.html)

### Inferences
- **Premium cues** with some evidence behind them: generous white space (older ad research), restraint and coherence (book-trend pieces), real or clearly hand-made imagery rather than AI people (Getty). **Cheap cues**: AI-looking humans or simulated product results (Getty; Etsy rules, Q3), generic stock or ad-like images (NN/g 2006), and cluttered heroes that fail at thumbnail size (Q1).
- **Match the type voice to the niche** (applies Schroll et al.; untested on PDFs). Functional, outcome-driven guides such as finance, budgeting, study and career should get machine type and Swiss or editorial layouts. Emotional or hedonic products such as wedding planners, baby memory books and travel journals can carry handwritten accents, scrapbook collage and paper texture.
- **Where the requested styles fit 2026:**
  - Puffy 3D and claymorphism-like textures fit Adobe's "puffy, soft, squishy" trend and Canva's Texture Check.
  - Risograph, grain and lo-fi fit Canva's "lo-fi aesthetic" (+527%) and the "imperfect" theme.
  - Editorial and magazine looks fit Canva Explorecore and Zinegeist and Adobe's freeform layouts.
  - Minimal Swiss fits Opt-Out Era (+54%) and Cloud Dancer.
  - Collage fits Notes App Chic (+90%).
  - Smooth glossy gradients appear in none of the 2026 lists I found.
- **Weight these as signals only.** Trend reports measure search growth on a platform, without baselines. They describe direction, not what converts.

### Gaps
- I found no measured test comparing these styles (collage vs minimal vs 3D, for example) on clicks or sales of digital products.
- I found no study that measures which 2025-2026 styles are perceived as premium vs cheap.
- The full Canva report was blocked (403), and Adobe's full report was not retrieved.
- Pinterest Predicts and Etsy's own trend reports were not checked.

## 3. Mockups (3D book, open spread, tablet, phone, laptop, flat lay, stacked bundle, "everything you get", page fans, printed pages): which convert better?

### Takeaway
I found no rigorous public data ranking mockup types for digital products. The few tests are small, and both favour showing the product in context:
- A 3D cover on an iPad eventually beat the flat cover in BookBub ads (Book Brush, 2021; CTRs not published).
- A room mockup with a person beat a white-background mockup in a vendor's Etsy poster test (2026). The conversion gain was not statistically significant.

Baymard confirms that shoppers use images to judge scale and format, which is the job a mockup does. Etsy forbids AI-simulated "results" for functional guides.

### Cited Findings
- [MEASURED, OLDER 2021] In the Book Brush/BookBub split tests (at least 2,000 impressions per variant), a 3D cover on an iPad overtook the flat 2D cover, and a plain solid background beat an enlarged-cover background. No CTR numbers were published. — [BookBub Insights](https://insights.bookbub.com/book-advertising-design-elements-clicks/)
- [ANECDOTE/VENDOR, 6 Mar 2026] George Jefferson, founder of Artomate, which sells mockup generation, tested Etsy posters. He swapped a plain white-background mockup for a living-room mockup with a person and a couch for scale. CTR went from 2.1% to 3.6% and conversion from 1.8% to 3.0%, on about 600 external clicks per version. The article contradicts itself on duration (two weeks vs three) and on the lift (40% vs 70%). — [Artomate](https://www.artomate.app/blog/ab-test-etsy-poster-listings-without-killing-rankings)
- [MEASURED, details paywalled] Baymard guideline #741, "Visually Indicating Product Scale", is rated "Essential": users rely on product images to judge scale. First observed May 2017, last updated Jan 2026. The percentages and sample sizes are behind Premium. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/guidelines/741-visually-indicating-product-scale)
- [MEASURED, secondary mirror, verify before citing] 56% of users first inspect images on a product page; 42% try to judge size from photos; 28% of sites provide no in-scale image. — [Baymard research article](https://baymard.com/research-articles/scale-mobile-product-images-in-landscape); [Baymard blog mirror](https://marketing-site-78zahjm59-baymard.vercel.app/blog/in-scale-product-images)
- [PLATFORM RULE] Etsy's image rules:
  - Functional guides (patterns, recipes, instructions) must show an actual result made with the instructions, not an AI-generated or simulated result.
  - If AI was used to edit listing photos, say so in the description.
  - At least one unedited photo of the actual item against a real background is required; some item types are exempt.
  - Editing must not create false expectations. — [Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015663347-Requirements-and-Best-Practices-for-Images-in-Your-Etsy-Shop)
- [PLATFORM RULE, OLDER 2022, via search excerpt] Etsy advises photographing digital items with the same care as physical ones, for example a framed print styled with props in natural light, or a project actually made from the pattern. — [Etsy Seller Handbook](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/47330319230)
- [PLATFORM RULE] Gumroad recommends a cover image of 1280 × 720 px at 72 DPI or more, and additional images of at least 600 × 600 px. — [Gumroad Help](https://gumroad.com/help/article/60-adding-a-cover-image). A 600 × 600 square thumbnail is widely cited but not confirmed in Gumroad's documentation. — [Seller Image Exporter](https://seller-image-exporter.netlify.app/blog/gumroad-cover-image-size-guide)
- [PRACTITIONER, method undisclosed] Conversion benchmarks for Etsy digital listings: 1.5-4% "healthy" and 4-7% "strong" in one guide; a 1-3% general Etsy average in another. — [InsightAgent](https://www.insightagent.app/guides/etsy-digital-product-benchmarks-2026); [Gelato](https://www.gelato.com/blog/etsy-conversion-rate)

### Inferences
- **The channel's geometry decides the mockup.** Etsy's hero image is landscape or square and is cropped three ways; Gumroad uses a 16:9 cover plus a square thumbnail. A portrait A4/Letter cover therefore needs a landscape scene around it (tablet, 3D book, desk flat lay), with the cover centred in a safe zone that survives square, portrait and landscape crops.
- **Pick the mockup by how the product is used.** A tablet or phone says "use on a device"; printed pages on a desk or a clipboard say "printable"; a page fan or open spread says "lots of content". The hero should answer "what is this and how do I use it?" in one glance, which is the scale and format job Baymard describes.
- **Keep bundle and "everything you get" composites for secondary images.** They are collages, which Etsy discourages only as the first photo (see Q4).
- **No AI-simulated results.** For guides with tangible outcomes (recipes, crafts, fitness programmes showing a result), do not use AI-simulated outcome photos on Etsy. This is policy, not taste.
- **Test-size reality, my own calculation.** In Artomate's test, 1.8% vs 3.0% on 600 clicks means about 11 vs 18 orders: z ≈ 1.4, p ≈ 0.17, not significant. Detecting 2% vs 3% conversion with 80% power at α = 0.05 needs about 3,800 visitors per variant. For most shops, mockup tests should be judged on CTR or impressions-to-favourites, not on sales.

### Gaps
- I found no data comparing 3D book vs tablet vs phone vs flat lay vs page fan vs bundle stack on Etsy, Gumroad or sales pages.
- Vendor pages claim "30-60% higher conversion" from professional mockups without any method; they are not cited.
- Etsy's handbook article "7 Essential Types of Product Photos" returned 403, so its list could not be checked.

## 4. Etsy listing images for digital downloads: first image, count, order, text overlays, video; Etsy guidance and measured results

### Takeaway
Etsy is strict about the first image. It should be a single clean square or landscape image of at least 2000 px, with no text overlay and no collage, and its subject centred so that it survives every crop. The search visibility page checks it. The rest is open: up to 20 photos and 2 silent videos of 5-15 s, though some Etsy pages still say 10 photos and 1 video. Etsy's own digital examples add an image that states plainly that the product is a download. Buyer surveys put photos at or near the top of purchase factors.

### Cited Findings
**First image and size (official)**
- [PLATFORM RULE] Etsy Help, "Requirements and Best Practices for Images":
  - At least 2000 px in width and height recommended; the first photo must be at least 635 px or it ranks lower.
  - Files over 1 MB may fail to upload.
  - The first photo should be landscape or square, and it sets the shape of the photos that follow.
  - Keep the subject clear in the square, portrait and landscape thumbnail views; shoot wider than needed and centre the subject.
  - Convert to sRGB.
  - The same page also says "avoid square crops", which contradicts the square option. — [Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015663347-Requirements-and-Best-Practices-for-Images-in-Your-Etsy-Shop)
- [PLATFORM RULE, 26 Aug 2025, via search excerpt] To keep search visibility, avoid text overlays, collages, or a set of images in the first thumbnail. — [Etsy Seller Handbook, "The Anatomy of a Well-Crafted Etsy Listing"](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1347574487014); summary of the earlier version: [Listadum, Feb 2025](https://www.listadum.com/fr/blog/whats-new-in-etsys-guide-to-crafting-a-winning-listing)
- [PLATFORM RULE] Etsy search visibility page (announcement updated 8/22, photo section 8/30; the year is not shown on the fetched page and is probably 2025):
  - It checks only the first photo.
  - A collage is any photo that combines several images; it is discouraged as the primary image and fine for secondary ones.
  - Minimum 0.4 megapixels; shortest side 2000 px at 72 PPI recommended.
  - The first photo should not be too dark or too bright and should have accurate colour.
  - "Multiple high quality photos" count towards listing quality, which search takes into account.
  - The page still says "up to 10 photos and one video". — [Etsy Community announcement](https://community.etsy.com/t5/Announcements/Etsy-search-visibility-page-More-insights-than-ever-before/td-p/146420965)

**Photo count and video**
- [PLATFORM RULE, conflicting] Third-party tools report that Etsy raised the photo limit from 10 to 20 (dated Aug 2025) and later allowed 2 videos. — [List Perfectly](https://listperfectly.com/selling/etsy-20-images-per-listing-list-perfectly/); [Alura](https://www.alura.io/news/two-videos-per-listing). The search visibility page and older handbook text still say 10 photos and 1 video.
- [PLATFORM RULE] Listing videos:
  - 3-15 s in the Help Center, 5-15 s in the Seller Handbook; anything longer is clipped.
  - No audio plays.
  - Minimum resolution 500 px, ideally 1080 px or more; up to 100 MB; MP4 and MOV accepted.
  - Explore-feed videos are different: 2 s to 3 min, with sound. — [Etsy Help: How to Add Listing Videos](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360053206073-How-to-Add-Listing-Videos); [Etsy Seller Handbook: 3 types of listing videos](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/821069530001); [Explore videos](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1078521357565)
- [PLATFORM RULE, via search excerpt] Etsy discourages watermarks because they make photos unsuitable for features on Etsy. It suggests including the brand inside the image instead. — [Etsy Help: How to Protect Listing Images](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000338427-How-to-Protect-Listing-Images)

**Digital-specific examples from Etsy**
- [PLATFORM GUIDANCE, OLDER Feb 2022, via search excerpt]
  - Sewing-pattern shop Ohhh Lulu adds a stylised photo of the finished item, which "dramatically" cut the number of buyers who thought they were buying the finished product. A fifth photo states plainly that the listing is a downloadable pattern.
  - Another seller runs a subtle banner along the bottom of her listing images to say the product is a printable kit.
  - Etsy also recommends saying "digital download" in titles and tags.
  - Source: [Etsy Seller Handbook, "How to Sell Digital Downloads on Etsy"](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/47330319230)
- [ANECDOTE] A recent Etsy forum thread is titled "Customers purchasing digital file patterns expecting a physical…", which shows the confusion still happens. — [Etsy Community](https://community.etsy.com/t5/Technical-Issues/Customers-purchasing-digital-file-patterns-expecting-a-physical/m-p/147293988)

**Buyer data and device mix**
- [SURVEY] eRank 2024 buyer survey (1,000 buyers): nearly 70% rate listing visuals and descriptions "very important" to purchase. An earlier eRank survey had 83% ranking photos the most important part of shopping on Etsy. A "90% of buyers" figure attributed to Etsy circulates but could not be traced. — [eRank 2024](https://help.erank.com/blog/2024-etsy-buyer-survey/); [eRank earlier](https://help.erank.com/blog/what-buyers-find-most-important-when-shopping-on-etsy/)
- [PLATFORM DATA] The Etsy app alone was 42% of 2024 GMS. — [Etsy Q4 2024 results](https://investors.etsy.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/13/etsy-inc-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-results). All mobile was 68% of GMS in 2023, a secondary figure attributed to Etsy. — [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/etsy-users)

### Inferences
- **Proposed image order for a PDF guide on Etsy.** This is a synthesis of Etsy's rules and examples; it has not been tested.
  1. Hero: one clean image (cover on a tablet, or a 3D book in a simple scene), centred safe zone, no text.
  2. "Instant digital download — PDF, N pages, A4 + US Letter, no physical item shipped" as a clear banner image. This is the role of Ohhh Lulu's fifth photo, moved earlier.
  3. "Everything you get" overview. A collage is allowed here.
  4-7. Inside pages: table of contents, 2-3 representative spreads, one close-up of a worksheet or checklist.
  8. How it works: buy, download, print or open on a tablet or in an app.
  9. Use in context: a real filled-in example, printed pages on a desk.
  10. Brand or series cross-sell.
  - Plus one silent 5-15 s video flipping through the pages, opening on the cover.
- **Text overlays.** Keep them off image 1. On images 2 and later, Etsy sets no restriction, but text must be readable on a phone, given the app's 42% share of GMS.
- **Old vs new guidance.** The 2022 examples (a banner on every image) predate the 2025 first-photo guidance. Apply the format banner from image 2 onwards.

### Gaps
- Etsy has published no measured lift for photo count or for video; claims such as "7+ photos convert 40% better" have no source.
- I found no Etsy guidance on screenshots of reviews as listing images, and their policy risk was not checked.
- Several handbook pages returned 403, so details come from search excerpts.
- The current photo limit (10 or 20) should be confirmed in the listing editor.

## 5. "What's inside" previews (sample pages, TOC image, filled-in worksheet before/after, close-ups): do they raise conversion or refunds?

### Takeaway
No controlled study measures inside-page previews on Etsy or Gumroad against conversion or refunds. Etsy's own seller example says that showing the result, plus an explicit "downloadable" photo, sharply reduced confused buyers. Etsy's policy makes "not as described" the main refund route for digital items, so previews must match the delivered file. Academic evidence on free ebook samples is mixed: samples can both promote and cannibalise sales, and they matter less once a product has many good reviews.

### Cited Findings
- [PLATFORM RULE] Digital listings cannot be returned or cancelled. Orders that are not as described, or never delivered, may be covered by Etsy Purchase Protection. — [Etsy Help: How to Download a Digital Item](https://help.etsy.com/hc/articles/115013328108)
- [PLATFORM GUIDANCE, OLDER 2022] A stylised result photo plus an explicit "downloadable pattern" photo cut down on confused customers (Ohhh Lulu). — [Etsy Seller Handbook](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/47330319230)
- [PLATFORM RULE] Functional guides must show a real result, not an AI-simulated one. — [Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015663347-Requirements-and-Best-Practices-for-Images-in-Your-Etsy-Shop)
- [MEASURED, small sample, date not checked] A UC Berkeley School of Information paper found no significant effect of ebook previews on sales. The author notes the sample is small and comes from a single genre. The model allows previews either to promote or to cannibalise sales. — [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/69n9j5xs)
- [MEASURED/MODEL] Previews and reviews act as substitutes: the effect of a preview falls as the number of reviews and the average rating rise. Previews can also cannibalise paid purchases. — [IJERT](https://www.ijert.org/too-much-preview-too-little-sales)
- [MODEL, OLDER 2017] Sampling a whole book probably loses sales. The paper proposes a model for choosing which ebooks to sample. — [Redalyc](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/965/96549490006.pdf)
- [SURVEY/CASE, OLDER 2006] 30% of ebook shoppers leaf through sample pages before buying. — [MarketingSherpa](https://marketingsherpa.com/article/case-study/how-to-make-hundreds-thousands)
- [VENDOR, OLDER] LibreDigital, a digital publishing vendor, reported that chapter previews helped both print and ebook sales. — [InternetNews](https://internetnews.com/small-business/what-do-online-book-browsers-want)
- [PRACTITIONER] Preview images should represent the real quality of the product; some shops watermark only the previews. — [Shop Circle](https://shopcircle.co/blogs/news/reduce-refund-requests-digital-products-better-descriptions)

### Inferences
- **Show the structure, not the substance, for information guides.** Use a table-of-contents image, 2-3 representative pages and one close-up, cropped or partly masked. For tool-type products (planners, worksheets, trackers), value comes from using them, so showing a fully filled example costs little. This follows the cannibalisation findings, which are about information goods.
- **Previews matter most at launch.** Because previews substitute for reviews, they do the most work for new listings with no reviews. They can be cut back once reviews accumulate.
- **Accuracy is a refund control.** Since "not as described" is the refund route, every preview must be taken from the actual delivered file: same pages, same design, same page count.
- **Before/after worksheets.** For a filled-in worksheet, use a genuinely filled example (handwritten or typed), not an AI-rendered "result". This follows Etsy's functional-guide rule.

### Gaps
- I found no data linking previews to refund or dispute rates on Etsy or Gumroad, and no measured conversion effect of TOC images or before/after shots.
- I found no data on the impact of Amazon's "Look Inside".
- The Berkeley paper's year and sample size were not checked.

## 6. Variety: what makes a catalogue of many products from one brand look rich rather than repetitive?

### Takeaway
I found no measured data on catalogue variety. Practitioners agree on a two-layer system:
- A fixed series skeleton: title typography as the series "logo", a fixed position for title and author, the same grid and brand mark.
- Variable art direction per title: colour, image or motif.

One documented case rolled a single typography-led design across a whole series and saw free downloads of book 1 rise 40% (BookBub 2024; uncontrolled).

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER] Treat the title font as the series "logo": keep its typeface, size and placement across volumes. Lock the design of book 1 before the later ones. Images need not match if the other elements stay constant. — [The Book Designer: 8 strategies](https://www.thebookdesigner.com/book-series-covers/); [The Book Designer: consistency](https://www.thebookdesigner.com/consistency-in-book-series-design/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Colour can shift to signal a change of tone within a series while the rest of the look holds. Avoid changing style mid-series, and keep a branding guide so the look stays consistent over years. — [Spine Magazine](https://spinemagazine.co/articles/creating-cohesive-visual-consistency-when-designing-a-book-series); [Indie Author Blueprint](https://indieauthorblueprint.substack.com/p/book-cover-branding)
- [PRACTITIONER] Frequently cited example: the recurring keyhole motif that marks each book of Freida McFadden's *Housemaid* series as part of the set. — [The Book Designer](https://www.thebookdesigner.com/book-series-covers/)
- [ANECDOTE, measured but uncontrolled] One typography design applied across a whole series coincided with +40% free downloads of book 1. — [BookBub Insights](https://insights.bookbub.com/boosted-series-sales-testing-cover-designs/)
- [PRACTITIONER, 2026] Book-cover trend pieces stress restraint and brand coherence. — [Troubador](https://troubador.co.uk/blog/book-cover-trends)
- [PLATFORM RULE] Etsy notes that the first photo's shape sets the shape of the photos after it, and that matching shapes make browsing more cohesive. — [Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015663347-Requirements-and-Best-Practices-for-Images-in-Your-Etsy-Shop)

### Inferences
- **A brand system an automated designer can run:**
  - Constants across the whole catalogue: the logo or wordmark position, one title type family per series, the grid and margins, the hero-mockup format (for example, always a tablet at the same angle, or always a 3D book), the "digital download" banner template, and image-2-onwards templates.
  - Variables per product: the dominant colour (rotated across a palette so adjacent tiles in the shop grid differ), the focal motif or illustration, and the composition type (type-only, object, illustration, collage).
- **Sub-series per niche under one master brand.** Each niche can get its own style family. Rotating families across niches keeps the shop rich, and keeping each family fixed within its niche keeps it recognisable.
- **Style families for the designer.** Each is grounded in the sources above; fit and risk are my judgements.

### Gaps

- I found no study of perceived "richness" vs "repetition" in multi-product shop grids.
